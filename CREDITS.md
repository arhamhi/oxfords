# Credits

Oxfords is a synthesis. The rules are written in its own words, but the ideas came from
people who did the thinking first.

## Kole Jain

The usability core of Oxfords (build order, hierarchy, edges, letting the data pick the
component, the hidden layer of states, dashboard and mobile structure) was learned from his
YouTube videos on UI and UX. If Oxfords helps you, watch the source:
https://www.youtube.com/@KoleJain

## Skills Oxfords learned from

| Skill | Author | Licence | What Oxfords took |
|---|---|---|---|
| [impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable) | Paul Bakaus | Apache 2.0 | The idea of a machine-checkable list of tells, the calm rules for product UI, and much of the forms, errors and accessibility floor |
| [taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill) | Leonxlnx | MIT | Countable pre-flight checks, the one-accent and one-family discipline, and the copy bans |
| [apple-design](https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills) | Emil Kowalski | MIT | Response before animation, interruptible motion, spatial consistency, and reduced motion as gentler rather than absent |
| app-designer and web-designer | shared privately with the author | not published | The contrast floor, the fuller list of states, and the judgement tests |

No text, rule lists or code were copied from any of these. Where Oxfords disagrees with one
of them, that is a choice about scope (plain product UI), not a claim that they are wrong.
Each of them does things Oxfords deliberately does not.
