# Credits

Oxfords is a synthesis. The rules are written in its own words, but the ideas came from
people who did the thinking first.

## Kole Jain

The usability core of Oxfords (build order, hierarchy, edges, letting the data pick the
component, the hidden layer of states, dashboard and mobile structure) was learned from his
YouTube videos on UI and UX. The colour states (hover lighter, pressed darker, disabled
desaturated), dark mode as its own design, destructive red regardless of brand, and adapting
a brand colour that fails contrast come from "The 7 Color Mistakes that RUIN your UI
Designs". If Oxfords helps you, watch the source:
https://www.youtube.com/@KoleJain

## Skills Oxfords learned from

| Skill | Author | Licence | What Oxfords took |
|---|---|---|---|
| [impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable) | Paul Bakaus | Apache 2.0 | The idea of a machine-checkable list of tells, the calm rules for product UI, much of the forms, errors and accessibility floor, colour as roles in OKLCH, and a `DESIGN.md` the next session reads first |
| [taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill) | Leonxlnx | MIT | Countable pre-flight checks, the one-accent and one-family discipline, the copy bans, a one-line read of the brief before building, deferring to a published design system, and a redesign that protects what people already know |
| [apple-design](https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills) | Emil Kowalski | MIT | Response before animation, interruptible motion, spatial consistency, and reduced motion as gentler rather than absent |
| app-designer and web-designer | shared privately with the author | not published | The contrast floor, the fuller list of states, the judgement tests, and the principle that a build is rendered and measured, then reviewed by fresh eyes |

No text, rule lists or code were copied from any of these. The scripts are original. Where Oxfords disagrees with one
of them, that is a choice about scope (plain product UI), not a claim that they are wrong.
Each of them does things Oxfords deliberately does not.

## Also

- Charles Liu, "20 AI Design Slop Indicators" (Instagram, 2026), for most of the tells added
  in v2.1: tags and pills, excessive italics, overused dividers, bordered containers,
  over-detailed UI, verbose text, code breadcrumbs, monospace as costume, ALL CAPS subtitles,
  gradient backgrounds and full stops in titles. Oxfords left out the ones it disagrees with,
  like negative tracking on display type and squircles.
- Impeccable's command table and its scored audit gave the shape for Oxfords' commands and
  the `audit` command.

- Sajid, "The Easy Way to Pick UI Colors" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvPklRN0Tco), for
  neutrals as even lightness steps, every colour as a set of shades, OKLCH over hex, and the
  two-layer shadow.
- Apple's Human Interface Guidelines and the WWDC design talks, by way of apple-design.
- WCAG 2.2 for the contrast and target-size numbers.
- The name is from Kingsman: "Oxfords, not brogues."
